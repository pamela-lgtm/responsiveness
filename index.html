Responsiveness

The fastest way to show someone your standard is high.

We spent last week on high standards. Responsiveness is where that standard becomes visible to somebody else.

Nobody watches us do the careful work. They do not see the file we read twice, the vendor we vetted, or the lease clause we checked before answering. What they see is how long it took us to answer at all. For most clients and most teammates, that is the entire basis on which they decide whether we are good at this.

So the standard is simple: always respond, even when you do not have the answer.

You do not need the answer to reply. You only need to let them know their request landed with a person who is going to handle it.

Sometimes you need to research the question. Sometimes you are finishing something else that cannot be dropped. Both are fine. What is not fine is silence, because silence gets filled in by the other person, and they rarely fill it in generously. They do not think you are busy. They think they were forgotten.

An acknowledgment takes ten seconds:

“I have your question — I need to pull the file and I will come back to you today.”

“Got it. I am finishing something for a move-in this afternoon, then this is next.”

“I saw this. I do not have the answer yet, and I am finding out.”

Then do the thing you said you would do, when you said you would do it. An acknowledgment you do not follow through on is worse than no acknowledgment at all.

Know your audience, and know the priority

Not every request carries the same weight, and part of doing this well is being able to tell the difference quickly. A resident with no air conditioning in August is not the same as an owner asking a general question about next year's renewal. A teammate blocked and waiting on you is not the same as a message that says “when you get a chance.”

Read who is asking, read what it is costing them to wait, and sort your day accordingly. Then tell people where they sit. Someone who knows they are third in line and will hear from you this afternoon is not waiting anxiously. Someone who has heard nothing is.

Why this one matters to me

Responding quickly is a form of respect. It says that the other person's time counts, that their problem is real, and that they are not managing us as well as their property.

And it says far more about us than it does about them. Anyone can respond fast when they have the answer ready. Responding when you do not — when the honest reply is “I do not know yet, and I am on it” — takes a little more, and it is exactly the thing people remember.

This is also the one part of our work we fully control. We cannot control the market, a vendor's schedule, or what a resident does on a Saturday night. We can control whether someone waiting on us knows they have been heard.

Thank you for the care you put into this every day.
